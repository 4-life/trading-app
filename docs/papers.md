# Papers & Trading Modes

## Paper

A paper is one strategy running on one asset. The owner creates papers from the [dashboard](dashboard.md).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `single` (default), `scanner` or `averaging` — see [Kinds](#kinds) |
| `asset` | selected from the [assets](market-data.md#asset-universe) only; none (`*`) for a scanner |
| `timeframe` | `1m` / `5m` / `15m` / `1h` / `4h` — candle scale the executor trades this paper on |
| `leverage` | default `1.0` |
| `direction` | `long` / `short` / `both` |
| `mode` | `demo` / `live` |
| `executor` | the [strategy executor](strategy-executor.md) that runs this paper |
| `profitability_demo` | `0` on creation |
| `profitability_live` | `0` on creation |
| position management | averaging papers only: `initial_size_usd`, `add_leverage`, `add_size_usd`, `take_profit_pct`, `max_drawdown_pct` — see [Averaging Papers](#averaging-papers) |

`direction` limits which positions the executor may open for this paper: only longs, only shorts, or both.

## Averaging Papers

A paper of kind `averaging` holds a **long** through the dips, adding to it, until its strategy sells above the take-profit, or the give-up. With `direction` **short** it's the same, mirrored: it opens on its strategy's short entries, adds as the price rises against it, unwinds and takes profit on the way down, closes on the strategy's short exits above the take-profit, and gives up above the first entry (the backtest's candles go up to their high first, then down; the chart's reductions read "bought back"). With `direction` **both** (VWAP and Regime — the executors that trade both) each position takes the direction of the entry signal that opens it — a long or a short, one at a time, managed as above; the other direction's signals are ignored while it's open (no add, no reversal), and once it closes the next entry in either direction opens the next one (on the same candle too). A reopen in trend keeps the closed position's direction. Meant for assets the owner expects to recover (an index like QQQ or SPY, or BTC) when they judge a bottom is near: the owner only switches the paper on (enable) and off (disable); its executor picks the entry point, and the paper manages the position from there. Its parameters are required (except `add_size_usd`); single and scanner papers have none and trade exactly as their executor does. Shares are fractions in the API (`0.3` = 30%) and percentages in the dashboard.

| Parameter | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `leverage` | 10 | the first entry's leverage (the existing field) |
| `initial_size_usd` | 10 | the first entry's **margin**, fixed — instead of the [budget allocator](budget-allocator.md)'s share. The [limits](risk-engine.md#limits) still apply. |
| `add_leverage` | 3 | each **dip** add's leverage, **at most 5×** (a signal add uses the paper's `leverage`): its margin backs its size at this leverage. Adds come on their own — see **Adds** below |
| `add_size_usd` | 10 | each **dip** add's margin; empty = `initial_size_usd` (a signal add uses `initial_size_usd`) |
| `take_profit_pct` | 0.3 | the **minimum gain on margin** for a close: the position stays open until its strategy's **sell signal** comes with the position's **whole PnL** — realized by its unwinds plus unrealized on the size held — at least this share of **the margin backing it** (`take_profit:<the strategy's reason>`) — not an exit of its own. With adds at 5×, +30% on margin is about +6% on price. |
| `max_drawdown_pct` | 0.4 | optional: close the whole position this far against its **first buy** (`max_drawdown`) — a tighter limit than the automatic give-up; ignored when it's past it |

**Add and give-up levels follow the exchange's liquidation:** the 50% (add) and 30% (give-up) margin left are where the margin call is at 20% left. An asset whose maintenance margin (a share of the notional, [market limits](market-data.md#market-limits)) liquidates sooner moves both up with it, kept at the same place between the liquidation and the entry — ENA's ~6% at 10× liquidates with 60% of the margin left (−4.3%), so the add comes at 75% left (−2.5%) and the give-up at 65% (−3.5%), instead of an add at −5% past the liquidation. Live, the backtest, the papers table's "gives up at" and the form's estimate (with the paper's asset known) all use it (`margin_levels`).

**The automatic give-up** (`out_of_margin`, always on): an add puts the margin left back above half; it only falls further once no add came — `max_margin_usd` reached, no free cash, a gap. At **30% left** (`GIVE_UP_AT_MARGIN_LEFT`) the position closes — before the exchange liquidates it at 20% left, saving the liquidation's fee and slippage. At a high leverage the exchange's maintenance margin (a share of the notional, [market limits](market-data.md#market-limits)) liquidates first — $750 at 50× with 1%: at −1.0%, before the give-up at −1.4% — and the backtest loses the **whole margin** there, as the exchange keeps what's left of it. Where that lands follows from the settings: the paper form shows it as you type ("Gives up at −9.4% from the first buy, after 4 dip adds ($5000 margin) - before the liquidation at −10.5%" for $1,000 at 30× with $1,000 adds at 3× and $5,000 max margin; $10 with $20 adds: −56.5%, 249 adds), and the papers table as "gives up at −X%". It counts dip adds only — signal adds and unwinds move it.
| `max_margin_usd` | 5000 | optional: the **most margin a position may lock at once** — the cash you actually have. Adds beyond it are refused live (the risk engine, with the "add rejected" Telegram warning) and skipped in the backtest, so a dip deeper than the cash covers runs on to the give-up or a liquidation, as it would for real. Empty = no cap |
| `compound_margin` | on | optional (off by default): **profits compound** — the max margin grows with the paper's realized PnL (losses shrink it): live, the risk engine's cap is `max_margin_usd` + the realized PnL of its closed positions in that mode; in the backtest a position's budget is all the cash, over `max_margin_usd` too. Off, profits are banked |
| `intraday` | on | optional (off by default): **intraday trading** — the position closes **as soon as** its whole PnL reaches `take_profit_pct` of its margin (`take_profit`, no strategy sell needed), and the paper **keeps running** after it: the strategy's next entry signal opens the next position. Off, the take-profit is only a floor under the strategy's sell and the paper stops after it |
| `reopen_in_trend` | on | optional (off by default), `intraday` only: after a take-profit close, a **fresh position** (the initial size, no adds) opens at the **close of the candle the take-profit hit on** while the strategy's **trend lasts** — its own trade (its backtest's, the same rules) still open there: Supertrend not switched, VWAP / Regime not sold since their last buy. Again after every take-profit while the trend lasts (`reopen_in_trend` entry). Live, checked on the first pass after that candle closes, by replaying the strategy over the stored candles; a reopen missed or rejected then isn't caught up. Off (or not intraday), nothing changes |
| `backtest_start` | 2026-03-15 | optional: where its [backtest](#averaging-papers) starts trading; empty = the whole stored history |

- **Long only** — `direction` must be `long`.
- **Low-margin warning** (demo and live) — once an approved add leaves room for **one more dip add or none** — under `max_margin_usd` (with `compound_margin`: + the realized PnL) and in the free balance, whichever is less — Telegram warns once, at that add: "paper 7 BTC (live): 1 more add left before its max margin - margin $900.00 of $1000.00, free balance $4,000.00. Add cash and raise the paper's max margin…". A deposit on the exchange alone doesn't raise the paper's cap: raise **Max margin** in its Edit dialog too (at once, an open position included).
- **Its own risk limits** — the [risk engine](risk-engine.md#responsibilities) holds an averaging paper to its `max_margin_usd` and `max_drawdown_pct`, not the account-wide ones: its buys aren't stopped by the account drawdown (15%) or daily-loss (5%) limits, the position / exposure limits or a risk block, and it isn't auto-disabled by the realized-loss limit. Still in force: the paper being enabled, the watchdog kill switch (the system itself unhealthy), another paper holding the asset, and the free cash. Its unrealized loss does still count in the account equity, so a deep dip can trip those limits for the other papers.
- **Stops after a take-profit close** (not `intraday`) — once a sell at the take-profit has closed the position, the paper disables itself ("take-profit reached - enable it again at the next bottom", alerted on Telegram); the owner enables it again at the next bottom. A give-up (`max_drawdown`) doesn't stop it: its executor may enter again.
- **Where it runs** — in the shared executor loop (`backend/app/executors/base.py`, rules in `position_management.py`), not in the strategies. While flat, the paper's strategy decides the entry as always. With a position open, the loop checks the give-up, then the next dip add, then the take-profit, then a signal add, every pass (5 s) against the **latest trade price** (the last closed candle's close if no trade arrived in the last 60 s).
- **Holding through the dips** — the strategy's exits (sell signals, stops, trailing stops, regime flips…) close the position **only with the PnL at least `take_profit_pct` of the margin backing it**; any other sell is ignored. Otherwise only the give-up, a [liquidation](execution-engine.md#liquidation) or a manual close end it. A price that passes the floor and falls back under it before a sell keeps holding until a sell comes above it again.
- **Adds — decided by the system** — a unit is added whenever the position's loss has taken **half of the margin backing it** (50% left, `ADD_AT_MARGIN_LEFT`) — halfway to the [margin call](execution-engine.md#liquidation) at 20% left. Each add brings more margin than risk, so the margin left jumps back up and the next add comes only when the loss catches up again: shallow dips get few adds, deeper ones more and more often, exactly as the margin call gets close (first $10 at 10× ≈ an add at −5%, −8.8%, −11.9%, −14.4%…).
  - An add is an `add` request in the [order queue](postgres.md#trading-decision-log), sized by the paper (`add_size_usd` × `add_leverage`) and checked by the [risk engine](risk-engine.md#responsibilities) for free cash. One at a time: a dip add queued since the latest buy and the latest closed candle isn't queued again — a rejected one (no free cash) is retried once per candle. A signal add comes at most once per closed candle, and not on the candle the position opened on — its entry signal holds for the whole candle, so a filled one would otherwise add again on the next pass. The two kinds don't hold each other back. The **first rejected add of a position** is alerted on Telegram (a warning: averaging is paused, top up the account), once per position, not on every retry.
  - **Signal adds** — while a position is open, every **entry signal** of its strategy adds a unit, **above or below the entry**, sized like the first entry (`initial_size_usd` at the paper's `leverage`, `signal_add`); at most one per candle. The dip adds then hold off the margin call if the price falls, the unwinds sell the units bought under the start on the way back up, and the take-profit closes the position. A signal add bought under the first buy's price is a dip unit too (unwound on a bounce).
  - **Unwinding (always on, a grid)** — on the way back up, each **dip unit** (a buy under the first buy's price) is sold at the **level of the add above it**: the lowest unit once the price is back at the next-higher unit's buy price, the highest one at the first buy's price — **except the lowest unit held: it's kept to the position's close** (the cheapest buy, the most profit at the take-profit), so the grid sells from the second-lowest up; a new lower add becomes the kept unit and frees the previous one to be sold — each **a little above that level** to cover its costs and leave a small gain: + 0.4% (`UNWIND_MARKUP`: slippage and the gain) + both fees (Lighter: +0.4%; Hyperliquid's 0.045% taker fee per side: +0.49%) (a partial close, reason `unwind_adds`, `close_fraction` = the unit's share of the size). So a bounce inside a deep dip already sells its cheaper units (all but the lowest); a new dip adds again, the next bounce sells again. The first unit and the signal adds bought above the start are kept. Live, the units still held are rebuilt from the position's orders (every sell took the lowest unit but the lowest). Each sale releases its share of the margin by size, as the exchange does for a partial close. The exchange books a sale against the **average entry**, so a unit sold under it reads as a small realized loss there; the chart's hover box and the positions table show each sale's **unit profit** (its size × sell price − its buy price). The backtest sells units on a candle's way up, but not in a candle that added units (its high may have come before its low). Telegram shows each sale as a PARTIAL CLOSE; the chart, a cyan "sold $" arrow above the candle; the positions table counts adds / sells and sums the sold units' profit. In a strong V recovery the cheaper units are sold before the move — all but the lowest, which rides it.
  - **Add leverage decides what holding costs.** Down to a −40% give-up from $10 at 10×: adds of $10 at 3× take 40 adds and $410; at 5×, 166 adds and $1,670; at 10×, over 9,000 adds and ~$96,000. Hence the 5× cap. Live, adds stop once the free cash runs out, and then the position can be liquidated.
- **The position** — each add grows it: size + the add's, the average entry recomputed, margin + the add's margin, the whole position's leverage = notional at entry ÷ margin. Adds at a lower leverage than the first entry lower it, so the liquidation price falls faster than the price. Profitability divides by all the margin put in.
- **Backtest** (temporary, `python -m app.backtest.averaging [YYYY-MM-DD]`, run when the paper is [prepared](#preparation) and again whenever it's edited; it starts at the given date, else `backtest_start`, else the whole history): the paper's executor gives the entries and the sell signals (the long entries and exits of its own backtest, replayed without saving; an executor without a backtest gives none) — plus every candle it signals a long on while its own backtest trade is open (as live asks it for an entry on every candle; VWAP and Regime's trend signal). From each one, the averaging rules manage the position candle by candle:
  - Inside a candle the price goes **down to its low first** (worst case). Every add level (where the margin left falls to half), the give-up and the **liquidation** price it reaches fire in price order: an add fills at its level, while the give-up and liquidation close the position there. A candle that gaps past a level fills at its open.
  - A position still open then closes at a **sell signal** of the strategy on that candle (its backtest's exit, at that exit's price) if the PnL there (before fees) — the position's whole PnL, its unwinds' realized PnL included — is at least the take-profit share of the margin backing it; a sell below it is ignored. (The unwinds don't move the average entry, so the size held can be above it while the position is in loss overall — that position waits.) Still open, an **entry signal** of the strategy on that candle is a signal add (at that entry's price, sized like the first entry).
  - **Unwinding — backtest only, for now**: the backtest's grid differs from live. Its units are the first buy, every dip add (whatever its price against the first buy) and every signal add under the first buy; on a candle's way up each one but the lowest held is sold **as soon as it's in profit** — at its own buy price + the unwind buffer (0.4% + both fees), lowest first — so every bounce banks small gains against the loss. With N dip units + the first buy, all but the lowest can be sold; the lowest rides to the close. **From 3 dip units held** (the first buy not counted) they sell **in pairs**: the two cheapest above the lowest, together in one sale, once the price is back at the higher one's buy price + the buffer — N dip buys, about N/2 sales; under 3, one by one at each unit's own price.
  - Adds stop at the **budget** if `max_margin_usd` is set (live: also when the free cash runs out); without it the backtest assumes the cash for every add is there. The cash starts at `max_margin_usd` and every closed position's PnL is added to it — a liquidation or give-up shrinks it, wins grow it — but a position may lock **at most `max_margin_usd`**, never more: profits are banked, not put back into averaging down. So the budget of a position is the cash, capped at `max_margin_usd` — with `compound_margin` not capped: the wins go back in; once it's under `initial_size_usd` no position opens (the account is drained). A liquidation after enough wins still leaves cash to go on.
  - After a take-profit, the next entry signal opens the next position, as if the owner re-enabled the paper.
  - **Slippage**: every fill — the entry, each add, each unwind and the close — is 0.05% against the position (`FILL_SLIPPAGE`: a buy higher, a sell lower; a short mirrored), so settings that only survive on the exact levels don't, in the backtest and the sweep alike.
  - The automatic give-up closes at 30% of the margin left, before the liquidation (the execution engine's rule: the margin down to `MAINTENANCE_MARGIN_PCT` of it) — which only a candle gapping past both still reaches (filled at the open, as the give-up).
  - **Stored one row per position**: `entry_price` = the average entry, `stop_loss_price` = the give-up level, `entry_reason` = the strategy's reason + the number of adds, `exit_reason` = `take_profit:<the strategy's reason>` / `max_drawdown` / `out_of_margin` / `liquidation`, `margin_usd` = its **peak margin** — the most it locked at once, the cash it really needed (with unwinds, re-buys recycle the same money, so a sum of every buy's margin would overstate it), `pnl_usd` = the PnL net of fees. `return_pct` = PnL ÷ that peak margin ÷ the paper's leverage, so the risk engine's loss limit, which multiplies by the leverage, reads **% of margin**. Each buy (the first entry and every add: candle, price, margin, notional) goes to `backtest_fills`. Fees: the provider's maker fee on every fill.
  - **Chart** — every buy is an amber marker at its price, labelled with its margin (`$10` the first entry, `+$10` an add); the violet circle is the close; a liquidation or a give-up (`max_drawdown`, `out_of_margin`) also draws a red dashed vertical line across the chart at its candle. Hovering a candle shows the position's adds, average entry → close, its peak margin and the result in $ and % of margin — and every buy on that candle with the **position right after it**: margin so far, average entry, the whole position's leverage, its **liquidation price** and its PnL at that price. Below the chart, a **positions table**: one row per position — opened, closed (or still open), adds, **peak margin**, **position value in $** (the notional of all its buys at entry → the same size at the close or last price), average entry → close, PnL in $ and % of margin, exit. With a position still open, the summary adds a **Total**: the closed positions' PnL + the open one's unrealized PnL. The summary above the chart is in $: positions, won, net PnL, the most margin any position locked at once (and the net as a % of it), adds per position, exits by reason, and per month.
  - **A position still open on the last candle is stored too** (`open_at_end`, valued at the last close, unrealized; its fills included). The chart shows it as open (no close marker, "still open" in the hover box), and the summary shows it apart as **Open now** (since when, adds, average entry → last price, margin, unrealized PnL). It's left out of the closed-position totals, the paper's backtest return and the risk engine's loss limit.
  - The executor's own backtest (e.g. `python -m app.backtest.regime`) skips averaging papers.
- **Settings sweep** (temporary, `python -m app.backtest.sweep <out.csv>`): which averaging settings do best, without saving papers or backtest rows. For every asset in `ASSETS`, a 1h Regime (not intraday) and a 15m VWAP (intraday) paper, each alone with its own $5k budget, $10 initial, $20 adds, the automatic give-up only; every leverage × add leverage × take-profit in its grids. Candles are backfilled first. The history is split 70/30: settings must be positive with no liquidation on the first part (train) and are ranked by the PnL on the rest (test). Writes one CSV row per run to `<out.csv>` (in a container a writable path such as `/tmp/out.csv` — the services run as an unprivileged user) and logs the top 5 per asset and strategy, and the best settings over all assets per strategy. **One paper** (the API's [`POST /papers/{id}/sweep`](api.md), the papers table's **Sweep** dialog, with its own max leverage and max margin — run in the background, every run's stats stored in `paper_sweeps` / `sweep_runs` (the paper's latest sweep only, one sweep at a time on the server), read sorted by any stat with `GET …/sweep/runs`): that averaging paper's executor, timeframe, add leverage, add size, give-up and max margin fixed; every leverage (3–100×, up to the asset's [max](market-data.md#market-limits)) × initial size ($5–$750) × take-profit (1–200% of margin) × mode — **hold** (the strategy's sell, the take-profit as its floor), **intraday**, **intraday-reopen** (`reopen_in_trend`) — × **compounding on / off** (`compound_margin`) over the same period as its backtest (from `backtest_start`, else the whole stored history — so the two compare), each run's **drained** date stored (its cash under the initial size: it stopped trading there; drained runs rank after the others), one process per leverage x initial size, each run's train / test split stored; the dialog shows the best 5 of each — **Fixed budget** and **Compounding**. The worker processes run at a low CPU priority (nice 10): the API and the executors come first. **Batch sweep**: several averaging papers' sweeps one after another, each on the same budget (not shared) and up to its asset's max leverage, replacing their previous sweeps — for an overnight run ([`POST /sweeps/batch`](api.md), `sweep_batches`, its latest only); no other sweep starts while it runs, and a paper's own Stop skips to the next.

## Stop-loss and Take-profit

A **single** paper can set its own **stop-loss** (`stop_loss_pct`) and **take-profit** (`price_take_profit_pct`), each a share of the **entry price** (0.02 = 2%; empty: none) — on top of whatever its strategy does. The position closes at the **first** of them and its strategy's own exits (signals, stops, trailing stops): a strategy without exits of its own (VWAP, Supertrend) gets them added; one with its own stops (Regime, Trend) keeps them, and the paper's tighter stop cuts in first. Averaging papers have their own take-profit and give-up instead — not allowed there (`422`).

- **Live** (demo and live): the [shared executor loop](strategy-executor.md) checks every open position against them every pass (5 s), on the latest trade price, **before** asking the strategy, and queues the exit (`paper_stop_loss` / `paper_take_profit`). An entry records their prices on its decision — the tighter stop and the nearer take-profit, against the strategy's own (`backend/app/executors/paper_exits.py`).
- **Backtest**: each of the strategy's trades is cut at them where one is reached before its own exit — from the candle after the entry, inside each candle the stop first (worst case), then the take-profit; each fills at its level (at the open past it), a stop `STOP_SLIPPAGE` further. Saving a paper's stop-loss / take-profit re-runs its backtest. An approximation: after such a cut the backtest waits for the strategy's next trade — live, a new entry signal may come before the strategy's own exit would have.
- **Sweep** (the papers table's **Sweep** dialog, [`POST /papers/{id}/sweep`](api.md)): every leverage (3–100×, up to the asset's [max](market-data.md#market-limits)) × stop-loss (none, 0.5–10%) × take-profit (none, 1–30%) on the paper's strategy trades over its last year. Each run cuts them at its stop-loss / take-profit and compounds them at its leverage (each trade on the whole margin, net of both maker fees, as the [chart's summary](dashboard.md#strategy-chart)), with the max drawdown and the train / test split. A trade reaching the exchange's **liquidation price** before its stop loses the whole margin — with it the equity — so a leverage too high for a stop shows. Runs stored in `exit_sweep_runs`, read sorted with `GET /papers/{id}/sweep/exit-runs` (`backend/app/backtest/exit_sweep.py`).

## Editing

`PATCH /papers/{id}` (the dashboard's **Edit**) replaces the paper's `leverage` and, for an averaging paper, its position management. Position management takes effect at once, on an open position too. The leverage changes only without an open position or an order in flight (the open position was opened at the old one), and live it must be a whole number. Everything else (asset, timeframe, executor, direction, mode) is fixed at creation. An averaging paper's backtest re-runs within the save (its trading doesn't pause); meanwhile the Save button reads "Saving, backtesting…" and its chart shows "Running the backtest…".

## Kinds

- **`single`** — one strategy on one asset, as above. Every executor trades single papers only.
- **`averaging`** — a **long, short or both** single paper that manages its own position: a fixed first size, automatic **averaging down**, a take-profit floor under its strategy's sell signals and a give-up — see [Averaging Papers](#averaging-papers). Its executor finds the entry; it's prepared (backfill, backtest), traded and charted like a single paper.
- **`scanner`** — a strategy that picks its coins itself across the market. It has no asset and never trades: it creates `single` papers (`parent_id` = the scanner) for the coins it finds, and those trade like any other paper — the executor, risk engine, execution engine and manual close don't know the difference. Executors that can scan: `trend`.

## Scanner Papers

The `scanner` service handles every scanner paper, after each candle close at its timeframe:

1. **Market** — the top `SCANNER_TOP_N` (default **40**) Hyperliquid perpetuals by 24h notional volume (delisted excluded).
2. **Candidates** — the coins whose last closed candle meets the executor's own entry rule (for `trend`: a [20-day breakout](trend-strategy-engine.md#signals), shorts only in a BTC bear phase), in a direction the scanner's `direction` allows. Candles come from Hyperliquid's REST API — no WebSocket.
3. **Children** — for each candidate, while the scanner has fewer than `SCANNER_MAX_ACTIVE` (default **10**) enabled children, best (most traded) first:
   - its history is backfilled (`BACKFILL_DAYS` of candles at the timeframe, and funding);
   - a `single` paper is created with the scanner's executor, timeframe, direction, leverage and mode — or the coin's old child re-enabled, if the scanner itself disabled it (never one the owner or the risk engine disabled).
   The executor then enters on that same breakout candle. Each creation / re-enable is alerted on Telegram.
4. **Cleanup** — a child with no open position and no order in flight whose coin isn't a candidate any more is disabled (`disabled_by = scanner`), keeping its history. A disabled scanner opens nothing and disables its children once they're flat.

- The scanner paper doesn't count in the [budget split](budget-allocator.md); each enabled child does, like any paper.
- [Market data](market-data.md#collected-data) collects candles and funding for every enabled paper's coin, so children don't need to be in the asset list.
- **Live without a click:** once an [exchange API](dashboard.md#exchange-api) is set, the coins a scanner picks are created (or re-enabled) as **live** papers at once — a trend doesn't wait for a demo track record (scanner coins have no backtest). Without an API, or with a fractional leverage, they're demo. They trade only while live auto-trading is on; one enabled live paper per asset still applies (a coin already live elsewhere is skipped). Their "Go live" button stays off.
- Deleting a scanner keeps its children (their `parent_id` is cleared).

## Preparation

A new `single` paper isn't traded straight away: its data is prepared first (`backend/app/papers/prep.py`, a loop in the market-data service — it holds the exchange connection and owns the data). `prep_status` walks:

1. **`backfilling`** — `BACKFILL_DAYS` of candles at the paper's timeframe (plus BTC at that timeframe for `trend`, its bear filter), funding, and features.
2. **`backtesting`** — only for executors with a backtest (`vwap`, `regime`, `supertrend`); `trend` skips it.
3. **ready** (`null`) — the executor starts trading it.

- An error leaves **`failed`** with `prep_error`; such a paper isn't traded — delete it and create it again.
- While `backfilling` / `backtesting` the paper **can't be deleted**, and the executor skips it. The status is in the database, so a restart resumes where it was.
- Papers a [scanner](#scanner-papers) creates skip this — the scanner backfills their candles itself.
- The dashboard shows the step with a spinner in the papers table and on the [chart](dashboard.md#strategy-chart).

## Lifecycle

1. A new paper always starts in **demo** and **disabled** (`disabled_by = owner`, "new paper - enable it to start"), regardless of the mode other papers run in. A single paper is still [prepared](#preparation) right away (backfill, backtest), so its backtest can be checked before it's enabled; a scanner paper scans only once enabled.
2. Once `profitability_demo` is above 0% PnL — **or its backtest is profitable** (net % of price after fees above 0, the [chart summary](dashboard.md#strategy-chart)'s figure) — the owner can switch it to **live**:
   - the owner must close the paper's open demo positions manually first;
   - the paper's leverage must be a whole number, and an [exchange API](dashboard.md#exchange-api) must be set in settings;
   - only one enabled live paper per asset (demo allows several, but only one of them can hold the asset at a time — see [risk-engine](risk-engine.md)). While an asset is live, no other paper can go live on it; the slot frees up only after the owner or the risk engine disables that live paper.
3. A paper is disabled only:
   - manually by the owner — also by closing its position by hand (the positions table's **Close**: disabled once the exit is placed, "position closed manually"), or
   - by its [scanner](#scanner-papers) once it's flat and its coin isn't a candidate any more, or
   - by the [risk-engine](risk-engine.md) when the paper's realized loss since it was created or last re-enabled (`enabled_at`) reaches its **loss limit** — 10% of its budget, or more for a paper whose backtest had deeper drawdowns (see [risk-engine](risk-engine.md)).

`profitability_demo` / `profitability_live` = the paper's realized PnL ÷ margin used by its closed positions in that mode, as a percentage — recomputed on every position close (by the [execution-engine](execution-engine.md), which closes positions).

## Modes

Demo and live run at the same time; each mode has its own budget, managed by the [budget-allocator](budget-allocator.md).

- **Market data** always comes from Hyperliquid's public endpoints, in both modes.
- **Demo**: order placement and PnL are simulated. Available balance is assumed to be **$5000**.
- **Live**: real orders on Hyperliquid, using the Hyperliquid API key from the app's settings.
- The Hyperliquid API key is optional. Without it everything runs in demo mode without errors; wherever live balance can't be read, the dashboard shows "no live data" instead of an error.

## Demo Reset

The owner can reset demo state at any time. Reset clears everything in demo: balance back to $5000, all demo positions (open and closed), demo orders, demo trading log entries, demo equity history, and every paper's `profitability_demo` back to 0. Live state is never touched.
